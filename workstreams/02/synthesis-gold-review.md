## Exact Gold-scoped replacement copy offered to 08

The following five replacements carry the Gold qualifications. Sentences about Bitcoin and After Gold remain proposals for their volume editors, not accepted facts from this pass. Existing routes can be retained as topical reading destinations; link relevance is not claim verification.

**Three jobs/coin:** Money can serve several jobs. A unit of account states prices, wages or debts in a specified arrangement. A means of payment can transfer value in an exchange; final settlement depends on the arrangement. An asset held for later use may store value, though its purchasing power can change. One object can perform several jobs, and the jobs can also separate. Evidence that gold is held does not show that wages are priced in it. Evidence that a merchant accepts an asset does not establish the terms of credit built around it. The early Gold chapters show ornament and weighed-metal roles and, in some regions and periods, coin and monetary-standard roles. The Bitcoin chapters should test those roles separately from price appreciation. Read [metal to money](/gold/03-from-metal-to-money-weights-rings-coins/) and [Bitcoin's demonstrated uses](/bitcoin/02-what-bitcoin-solved-and-what-it-did-not/).

**Metal/institutions/claims:** Gold's monetary role depended on more than its physical properties. People could weigh, assay, divide and carry it, while issuing authorities and markets set coin standards and acceptance rules. Silver and copper often handled smaller transactions; notes and bank deposits were distinct claims with their own legal terms. Under a specified gold-convertible arrangement, a note could be redeemable at a defined parity, and banks could also issue claims against fractional reserves. That creates a conditional question: if redeemable claims grow relative to available reserves, how will the issuer meet conversion requests? The bimetallism and gold-standard chapters describe different arrangements and strains rather than one universal metal-backed ladder. Read [other metals and fixed ratios](/gold/05-silver-copper-bronze-and-bimetallism/) and [the gold-standard era](/gold/07-the-gold-standard-era-1717-1971/).

**1971/Triffin:** In August 1971 the United States suspended the Bretton Woods promise to convert foreign official dollar holdings into gold. Paper instruments, bank credit and non-gold prices predated that suspension. Other exchange-rate arrangements changed in stages through the early 1970s, while the dollar's international uses continued. Inflation, oil shocks and policy negotiations overlapped in time; their causes cannot be assigned to the suspension alone. Triffin's analysis described a conditional tension within Bretton Woods: additional foreign official dollar claims held as reserves could support liquidity while straining confidence in gold conversion if no alternative reserve source supplied that growth. It was not a law that every reserve issuer must run a trade deficit or face a run. Read [why the dollar displaced gold](/gold/08-why-the-dollar-replaced-gold/) and [the break after 1971](/after/01-the-break-1971-1976/).

**Gold present role/denominators:** When major international currencies ceased fixed gold redemption, gold remained an asset held by monetary authorities and households. Holding it as a reserve is different from defining most wages, taxes and debts by a weight of gold; a stated reserve basket is also different from fixed-weight redemption. A market-value gold-reserve share can move because its price changes, so the share alone cannot identify active purchases or prove a gold standard has returned. Gold's share of broad official reserves that include gold and a currency's share of foreign-exchange reserves that exclude gold have different denominators. Trade invoicing, reserve portfolios, debt denomination and payment rails are separate measures; a shift in one does not establish a matching shift in the others. This copy quotes no live number. Any numeric version needs an accepted observation, period, price convention and source. Read [gold's present role](/gold/09-gold-today-what-still-holds-its-value/) and [recent monetary shocks](/after/09-pandemic-inflation-and-weaponized-reserves-2020-2026/).

**No single winner:** This history does not by itself rank every monetary arrangement. Physical gold remains an official reserve asset, while most contemporary wages, prices and debts are stated in national currencies. Fiat arrangements may give monetary authorities more room to respond to demand and crises, but outcomes depend on institutions, constraints and costs. Bitcoin's design and any proposed unit-of-account role need their own legal, financial and empirical review before being compared as an economy-wide alternative. A comparison should specify the use and risk being tested—payment, long-term holding, pricing, credit, crisis response or political control—and use matched evidence where a ranking is claimed. The final Bitcoin conclusion and comparison view remain dependent on editor 04 and package 09. Read [the final question](/bitcoin/13-is-bitcoin-the-answer/) and [compare arrangements](#/compare).
