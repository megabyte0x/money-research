## Takeaways proposed for reader use

| Article | Takeaways | Evidence | Uncertainty / revision condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bitcoin-01` | Participants can validate issuance and transfers under chosen rules. Confirmations lower replacement risk without a fixed finality hour. Later exchange and custody layers require separate trust. | Whitepaper; Bitcoin Developer Guide, “Verifying Payment.” | Genesis headline does not prove full author intent; dated node, price and market counts need accepted observations. |
| `bitcoin-02` | Validation checks rules; a key authorizes spending under an output's conditions. Self-custody removes an account provider's freeze power but leaves inclusion, access and coercion risks. Payment use needs a Bitcoin-specific denominator. | Bitcoin Developer Guide; Bitcoin Core UTXO RPC. | Event amounts, global Bitcoin payment share and comparable remittance costs remain open. |
| `bitcoin-03` | A cryptocurrency survey is not a Bitcoin owner census. Exchange purchases, cross-border flows and payments measure distinct channels. Lightning public capacity does not measure spending. | Federal Reserve 2025 SHED table 33; Chainalysis Nigeria purchase comparison; IMF Nigeria annex VII. | Global Bitcoin owner/payment shares and overlapping fund, custody and company totals need same-date definitions. |
| `bitcoin-04` | Payment law, public holding, state-linked mining and private access are different policies. El Salvador amended mandatory private acceptance while retaining statutory “curso legal” wording. A seizure or complaint is not a finally forfeited reserve asset. | El Salvador Decree 199 and IMF CR 25/58; U.S. reserve order and DOJ complaint; DHI mining announcement. | Country outcomes, current holdings, sale/disposition and post-schedule CLARITY status need primary records. |
| `bitcoin-05` | Identify a speaker's role and document before treating a view as institutional policy. Staff research, a policy paper and a binding rule carry different authority. A reserve-order argument is narrower than a Bitcoin-standard argument. | ECB blog byline/disclaimer; IMF policy paper; Minneapolis Fed working-paper abstract; CNB test release. | Individual quotations, office dates and exact transcripts outside the reviewed core remain open. |
| `bitcoin-06` | Tender labels do not settle private acceptance, taxes or pricing. Domestic law determines debt discharge and public reserve authority. Bank exposure standards require local implementation checks. | El Salvador Decree 199; Basel SCO60. | Other domestic statutes, local Basel adoption and analyst-defined economic thresholds remain open. |
| `bitcoin-07` | Bitcoin's fixed base does not fix deposits or credit. Monetary effects depend on conversion promises, lending, velocity and output. A Gresham-style claim needs an enforceable ratio and an actual circulation arrangement. | Weber's conditional model; Bank of England money-creation work. | No observed Bitcoin-denominated national credit economy tests the forecast; dollar-stablecoin effects need native-contract measures. |
| `bitcoin-08` | Reserve comparisons require the holder's mandate, law and custody. COFER foreign-exchange reserves and broad official reserves use different denominators. No universal return or crisis-hedge rank follows from unmatched windows. | IMF COFER; ECB international-role report; scoped SEC valuation window. | Matched after-cost returns and reserve-stress evidence are not accepted; custody and coercion outcomes depend on arrangement. |
| `bitcoin-09` | Validators enforce chosen validity rules; miners propose blocks and influence inclusion. Custodians, keys, exchanges and law affect different uses of a coin. Dormancy does not prove a key is lost. | Bitcoin Developer Guide; Bitcoin Core UTXO RPC; SEC trust structure. | Owner, pool, chip and company totals overlap or change with date; a control map is not measured censorship. |
| `bitcoin-10` | Private access, public investment and central-bank reserve use need separate authority. A forfeited-asset reserve order is not a purchase order. A retirement-plan review order does not require Bitcoin access in every 401(k) plan. Native invoicing is a further empirical step, not a necessary adoption ladder. | U.S. reserve order; Executive Order 14330 §§2–3 and Labor's March 2026 proposed rule; CNB mixed-asset test release. | Country rankings, future sequence, actual public inventory and actual plan offerings need separate records. |
| `bitcoin-11` | Fixed base issuance is a protocol constraint. Fee-funded mining security is a scenario requiring revenue and attack assumptions. Draft BIPs are proposals, not activation deadlines. | Bitcoin Developer Guide; BIP-360/361 headers; dated miner report. | Live concentration, security-budget and local-law metrics need dated primary observations; Lightning capacity is not use. |
| `bitcoin-12` | Redeemable custodial claims and leverage can transmit run risks. Debt deflation depends on a specified monetary and credit regime. Historical analogies identify channels without proving identical national outcomes. | Weber model; historical and crypto-intermediary mechanism sources; BIP draft headers. | Spillover size, protections, issuer solvency and Bitcoin-denominated macro outcomes remain unmeasured. |
| `bitcoin-13` | Auditable issuance addresses one issuer-trust problem. Investment and settlement use does not establish native national denomination. A reliable inflation or crisis hedge needs matched, user-relevant comparisons. | Bitcoin Developer Guide; scoped SEC valuation; IMF staff correlation research. | Native wages/prices/debts, end-to-end payments, after-cost returns, state holdings and Zcash-specific evidence remain open. |

## Approved question, answer, evidence and uncertainty copy

| Article ID and slug | Short question | Approved short answer | Evidence and uncertainty to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bitcoin-01` · `01-the-origin-what-2008-produced` | What did Bitcoin's design add after the 2008 crisis? | It let participants verify issuance and transfers without a central ledger operator; dollar-priced exchanges, funds and custodians later formed around it. | Whitepaper and protocol design support the mechanism; the genesis headline alone does not establish Nakamoto's full intent, and dated network/market counts are open. |
| `bitcoin-02` · `02-what-bitcoin-solved-and-what-it-did-not` | Which trust problems does Bitcoin solve for a self-custodied user? | Independent validation and key-controlled transfer reduce reliance on an account provider, while confirmation risk, access, custody choices and price risk remain. | Developer documentation supports validation and probabilistic settlement; protest amounts, global Bitcoin payment share and matched remittance costs are unverified. |
| `bitcoin-03` · `03-how-bitcoin-is-actually-used-global-adoption` | How can Bitcoin's uses be measured without mixing populations? | Investment, custody and some payments are observed, but surveys, exchange purchases, on-chain flows and Lightning capacity answer different questions. | U.S.-adult cryptocurrency survey and Nigeria's channel-specific estimates have defined scope; global Bitcoin owner/payment shares and current industry totals do not. |
| `bitcoin-04` · `04-countries-that-adopted-bitcoin-and-what-happened` | What can a country mean when it says it has adopted Bitcoin? | A law on payments, a public asset holding, state-linked mining and regulated private access are different acts; none alone makes Bitcoin the national pricing unit. | El Salvador's amended statute and the U.S. reserve order establish legal distinctions; current holdings, country outcomes and the scheduled CLARITY motion require later records. |
| `bitcoin-05` · `05-supporters-and-opponents-and-their-reasoning` | Who is speaking for or against a Bitcoin standard? | A named official, staff author, policy paper, executive order and bank standard have different authority and address different proposed uses. | The reviewed ECB/IMF/CNB/Minneapolis core supports that distinction; many individual quotations and office dates still need exact transcripts. |
| `bitcoin-06` · `06-what-legal-tender-requires-and-what-is-missing` | Does calling Bitcoin legal tender make it the unit of account? | No. Debt-discharge rules, tax denomination, private acceptance, prices and reserve authority depend on the country's own law and actual use. | El Salvador's amendment provides one case; other domestic statutes, local Basel application and the chapter's economic thresholds remain open. |
| `bitcoin-07` · `07-the-supply-system-fixed-rate-variable-rate-and-backing` | Does a fixed Bitcoin base fix the whole money supply? | No. Deposits, lending, velocity and prices depend on the banking and redemption arrangement built around the base asset. | Bank-of-Canada-hosted Weber model and central-bank money-creation work support conditional mechanisms; no observed Bitcoin-denominated national economy validates a forecast. |
| `bitcoin-08` · `08-why-not-gold-again` | How should gold and Bitcoin be compared as reserve assets? | By the reserve holder's law, custody, liquidity, loss horizon and mandate, with the same dates and units for performance claims. | ECB/IMF reserve measures have different denominators; no accepted matched after-cost return or crisis-hedge series ranks the assets universally. |
| `bitcoin-09` · `09-supply-and-control-who-holds-bitcoin-and-who-benefits` | Who controls Bitcoin's rules, transfers and holdings? | Validators enforce their chosen rules, while miners, custodians, key holders, exchanges and law affect different steps in using the asset. | The protocol and fund structures support this control map; ownership, pool, chip and company totals are overlapping or date-sensitive and remain open. |
| `bitcoin-10` · `10-the-path-for-large-economies` | Is there one path from regulated access to a Bitcoin standard? | No. Private access, public investment, central-bank reserves, settlement and native invoicing need separate decisions and evidence. | The U.S. forfeited-asset order and Czech test establish limited examples; a ladder, large-country rankings and future adoption sequence are scenarios. |
| `bitcoin-11` · `11-defects-that-stop-bitcoin-from-being-a-global-currency` | Which limits come from Bitcoin's design and which can change? | Fixed base issuance is a protocol constraint; payment use, price stability, miner participation, credit and custody outcomes must be measured under an actual arrangement. | Developer documentation and draft BIPs establish technical scope; live concentration, security-budget and legal claims need dated primary records. |
| `bitcoin-12` · `12-how-bitcoin-could-reproduce-old-crises` | Could familiar financial runs form around Bitcoin? | Custodians, leverage and redeemable claims can transmit run or forced-sale risks, but analogies do not prove identical national crises. | Historical and crypto-intermediary cases identify mechanisms; spillover size, protections and Bitcoin-denominated macro outcomes remain unmeasured. |
| `bitcoin-13` · `13-is-bitcoin-the-answer` | What does the reviewed evidence show about saving, paying and institutional trust? | Bitcoin addresses some issuer and intermediary trust problems; broad native denomination and a reliable inflation or crisis hedge are not established here. | Protocol design and scoped market studies support a limited assessment; matched returns, payment populations, credit arrangements and Zcash-specific evidence remain open. |
