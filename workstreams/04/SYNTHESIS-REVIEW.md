## 1. Three monetary roles — QUALIFY

Original (verbatim):

> Money does several jobs at once. A unit of account states prices, wages and debts. A payment medium settles an exchange. An asset held for later use may store value. One object can perform all three, but the jobs can also separate. The research starts with that distinction because arguments about gold or Bitcoin often slide between them: evidence that something is held does not show that wages are priced in it, and evidence that a merchant accepts it does not show that a financial system can issue credit in it. The early gold chapters trace how metal moved from ornament and weight to coin and monetary standard. The Bitcoin chapters return to those roles rather than asking whether a rising price alone makes a new money. Read [metal to money](/gold/03-from-metal-to-money-weights-rings-coins/) and [Bitcoin's demonstrated uses](/bitcoin/02-what-bitcoin-solved-and-what-it-did-not/).

Replacement (full paragraph):

Money can serve several roles: a unit of account names prices, wages and debts; a medium of exchange helps parties pay; and an asset may be held to preserve purchasing power for later use. These roles can be performed by one instrument or split among assets and claims. Holding Bitcoin does not establish that contracts are natively priced in it, and accepting a Bitcoin payment does not show that a country's taxes, credit and wages are denominated in it. Nor does a rising asset price alone establish reliable long-term purchasing-power protection. The early gold chapters examine regional paths from valued metal to weighed payments, coins and later monetary standards, without implying one universal sequence. The Bitcoin chapters apply the same separate-role questions to protocol use and the institutions built around it. Read [metal to money](/gold/03-from-metal-to-money-weights-rings-coins/) and [Bitcoin's demonstrated uses](/bitcoin/02-what-bitcoin-solved-and-what-it-did-not/).

Reason: “Payment medium settles an exchange” risks blurring payer authorization, transfer and recipient settlement; “metal moved” sounds like a universal ladder. The replacement also prevents store-of-value from being inferred solely from appreciation. Gold-history specifics remain for editor 02 to approve.

## 2. Base validation and credit — QUALIFY

Original (verbatim):

> Bitcoin changes a different part of the arrangement. Its base issuance follows published consensus rules rather than a central bank's discretion, and users can validate the ledger without trusting one issuer to maintain it. These are important design claims, but a ledger rule does not decide how exchanges, custodians, banks, laws and merchants will behave around it. Bitcoin is used as an asset and sometimes for payment; becoming the unit of account of a large economy asks for much more. The volume examines legal tender, taxes, lending, exchange-rate options and what fixed base supply might mean for nominal debts. Some of those questions are scenarios rather than observations. Their answers depend on whether claims denominated in bitcoin could grow, whether they are redeemable, and who bears loss when redemption fails. Read [the origin and design](/bitcoin/01-the-origin-what-2008-produced/) and [a Bitcoin unit of account](/bitcoin/07-the-supply-system-fixed-rate-variable-rate-and-backing/).

Replacement (full paragraph):

Bitcoin changes the base-asset and validation rules of an arrangement. A participant running a validating node can check blocks and the permitted subsidy under the rules that node runs, without relying on one issuer's ledger. The schedule gives a maximum permitted issuance; it does not count inaccessible keys, liquid float or every satoshi a miner actually claims. These protocol properties do not decide how exchanges, custodians, lenders, merchants or domestic laws operate around Bitcoin. Investment and some transfer uses are observed, while broad native Bitcoin denomination of wages, prices and debts in a large economy is not established here. The volume therefore treats a Bitcoin-denominated economy as a set of conditional regimes: deposits and other claims could grow or contract; redemption, reserve coverage, lending of existing funds, credit risk and velocity would depend on the chosen arrangement. Fixed base issuance alone does not fix broad money, credit or the price path. Read [the origin and design](/bitcoin/01-the-origin-what-2008-produced/) and [a Bitcoin unit of account](/bitcoin/07-the-supply-system-fixed-rate-variable-rate-and-backing/).

Reason: A validator's rule check is more precise than a general user validating “the ledger”; height/schedule do not establish exact issued or spendable supply. The replacement names model assumptions from E07/E15 and withholds a national unit-of-account outcome.

## 3. Layers of control and country adoption — QUALIFY

Original (verbatim):

> Control is also plural. Protocol participants can reject a rule they do not accept, miners produce blocks, developers propose software, exchanges and custodians hold keys for clients, and public authorities set rules for activities within their jurisdictions. None of these actors alone has every lever. Measuring control requires specifying the lever and the date: ownership of coins is not identical to custody, mining-pool coordination is not identical to node validation, and an exchange-traded trust's assets are not additive to assets its custodian safeguards on the trust's behalf. The Bitcoin volume examines both supply and these institutional layers. Its country chapters explore different forms of adoption, from a reserve holding to a mandatory payment rule; those are not interchangeable steps toward a full Bitcoin standard. Read [supply and control](/bitcoin/09-supply-and-control-who-holds-bitcoin-and-who-benefits/) and [country experiments](/bitcoin/04-countries-that-adopted-bitcoin-and-what-happened/).

Replacement (full paragraph):

Control over Bitcoin depends on the action in question. Validating nodes choose which blocks satisfy their rules; miners and pools construct blocks and affect transaction inclusion; developers propose code; custodians and exchanges can restrict client access to keys or withdrawals; a key holder can be compromised or coerced; and public authorities govern activities within their legal reach. None of those powers is identical to complete control of every transfer. A coin's beneficial owner, an ETF trust, its shareholders and its custodian can describe overlapping interests in the same asset, while block-template coordination is different from node validation. Country “adoption” also needs a named legal act and public entity: required or voluntary payment acceptance, seized property, finally forfeited Treasury assets, state-linked mining, sovereign-fund investments and central-bank reserves are distinct statuses. They should not be added into an unverified national holding or arranged as inevitable steps toward a Bitcoin standard. Read [supply and control](/bitcoin/09-supply-and-control-who-holds-bitcoin-and-who-benefits/) and [country experiments](/bitcoin/04-countries-that-adopted-bitcoin-and-what-happened/).

Reason: E20/E28 require a fuller validation/inclusion/custody/coercion taxonomy and legal status distinctions. “From a reserve holding” could imply an audited contemporary holding; the replacement uses categories without a current figure or blanket immunity promise.

## 4. Claims and run analogies — QUALIFY

Original (verbatim):

> Bitcoin can reproduce problems familiar from earlier money systems if promises accumulate around it. A custodian can fail to return assets, a leveraged position can unwind quickly, and a claim called “bitcoin” may be backed by less bitcoin than holders expect. Those risks do not mean the base ledger itself has the same balance sheet as a bank. They mean a financial system built on top of a scarce base still has to specify custody, disclosure, redemption and loss allocation. A stablecoin creates a parallel question: its label does not establish which reserve assets exist, who has a direct redemption right or whether the issuer is solvent. Proof of reserves may show selected assets at one time without testing all liabilities. The crisis chapter maps these mechanisms as analogies, not as claims that every past crisis will recur. Read [old crises in a new system](/bitcoin/12-how-bitcoin-could-reproduce-old-crises/) and [the limits of a global currency](/bitcoin/11-defects-that-stop-bitcoin-from-being-a-global-currency/).

Replacement (full paragraph):

Financial claims built around Bitcoin can carry risks familiar from earlier systems. A custodian may be unable to return client assets, a leveraged position may be sold into a falling market, and a redeemable claim may fail if assets, liabilities or redemption rights are misunderstood. These are mechanisms of a particular intermediary or contract, not a balance sheet of Bitcoin's base ledger. A stablecoin is a token designed to target another asset's value; its label alone does not establish cash or Treasury backing, a direct redemption right for every holder, deposit insurance or issuer solvency. A proof-of-reserves report may address selected assets at one time, but what it establishes depends on its procedures and whether liabilities and asset rights were examined. The historical crisis comparisons identify possible run and contagion channels under stated arrangements; they do not claim that Bitcoin has reproduced each named national crisis or will do so at scale. Read [old crises in a new system](/bitcoin/12-how-bitcoin-could-reproduce-old-crises/) and [the limits of a global currency](/bitcoin/11-defects-that-stop-bitcoin-from-being-a-global-currency/).

Reason: The replacement keeps the intermediary mechanism conditional and aligns stablecoin/proof-of-reserves language with the narrowed Bitcoin glossary and E12/E17/E26 qualifications. It does not assert an unsupported aggregate shortfall or observed national Bitcoin macro crisis.

## 5. Final no-single-winner comparison — QUALIFY; synthesis remains withheld

Original (verbatim):

> There is no single winner implied by this history. Gold is a monetary asset without being the unit of most modern economies. Fiat systems can adapt to demand and crises, yet those adaptations depend on institutions and can fail or distribute costs unevenly. Bitcoin offers a rule-bound base and self-validation, yet its use as an economy's unit of account remains a proposal with unresolved legal and financial architecture. The comparison is clearer when each arrangement is tested against one use and one risk at a time: payments, long-term holding, pricing, credit, crisis response or political control. The final Bitcoin chapter asks whether it solves any one of these problems, rather than treating “the money problem” as a single verdict. Read [the final question](/bitcoin/13-is-bitcoin-the-answer/) and [compare arrangements](#/compare).

Replacement (full paragraph):

This history does not establish one monetary winner for every use. Gold remains an official and private reserve or savings asset without defining most modern wages and debts. State-issued money and bank claims can support credit and crisis-liquidity arrangements, but their results depend on governing institutions, balance sheets and law. Bitcoin permits independent validation of a rule-bound base asset and self-custodied transfers; those properties do not guarantee stable purchasing power, unrestricted inclusion or a workable national unit of account. A Bitcoin-denominated economy remains a model whose credit, legal, tax and crisis arrangements would need to be specified and observed. A responsible comparison asks a defined question for each arrangement—payment delivery, long-horizon purchasing power, contract pricing, credit, reserve liquidity or control—and uses matched evidence for that question. The final Bitcoin chapter lays out revision criteria rather than a universal verdict. Read [the final question](/bitcoin/13-is-bitcoin-the-answer/) and [the monetary regimes](/bitcoin/07-the-supply-system-fixed-rate-variable-rate-and-backing/).

Reason: The existing `#/compare` destination is provisional under package 09, so it should not be offered as a reviewed conclusion here. “Fiat systems adapt” is narrowed to institutions and specified claims; Bitcoin's protocol properties do not settle every practical use. A comparison view link can be restored only after its evidence matrix and copy are accepted.
