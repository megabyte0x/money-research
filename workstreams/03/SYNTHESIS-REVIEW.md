## 1971 break and Triffin — **QUALIFY**

Current paragraph, verbatim:

> The 1971 break was a change in one specific promise: official dollar conversion into gold under Bretton Woods. It did not instantly invent paper money or turn every price into a new currency. Exchange-rate arrangements, domestic banking and the dollar's international use continued, but they had to operate without that gold conversion rule. The first post-gold chapters follow the resulting negotiations, floating rates, oil shocks and inflation. These developments overlap in time; their causes should not be reduced to a single event. The Triffin problem is useful here as an analysis of the dollar–gold arrangement: foreign dollar claims could support international liquidity while putting conversion confidence under strain. It is not a universal law that a reserve issuer must always run a trade deficit or eventually face a run. Read [why the dollar displaced gold](/gold/08-why-the-dollar-replaced-gold/) and [the break after 1971](/after/01-the-break-1971-1976/).

Required replacement, verbatim:

> In 1971 the United States suspended foreign monetary authorities' conversion of official dollar holdings into gold under Bretton Woods. That change did not invent paper money or replace every domestic currency. Exchange-rate rules and banking arrangements evolved on separate tracks: major currencies moved toward floating rates, while the IMF's later amendment changed the international gold rules. Oil shocks and inflation overlapped with those changes, but inflation had already begun and cannot be assigned to the gold-window decision alone. The Triffin problem describes a conditional tension in the dollar–gold arrangement: foreign official dollar holdings used as reserves supplied liquidity, yet could grow relative to US gold available for official conversion and weaken confidence in that promise. It does not prove that a reserve issuer must always run a trade deficit or inevitably face a run. Read [why the dollar displaced gold](/gold/08-why-the-dollar-replaced-gold/) and [the break after 1971](/after/01-the-break-1971-1976/).

After Gold 01/02 approved summaries support the separated suspension, floating-rate and IMF changes and the pre-1971 inflation chronology. The Gold/Bitcoin canonical review supplies the foreign-**official** reserve-holder and conditional Triffin qualification. The replacement does **not** assert a measured claims-to-gold ratio, a legal change for every currency in 1971–73, or a one-cause inflation estimate. Editorial route relevance is accepted; evidence sign-off for individual dates and historic reserve stocks remains separate.

## Post-gold institutions and crisis responses — **QUALIFY**

Current paragraph, verbatim:

> The post-gold system rests on institutions with choices. Governments define legal obligations; central banks influence reserves and liquidity; commercial banks create claims through lending; payment systems carry balances between them. None of those choices is unlimited. Debt crises, financial bubbles, cross-border flows and shocks have repeatedly exposed weaknesses in the rules or in how they were applied. The research's chronology follows Latin American debt distress, emerging-market crises and the 2007–09 crash, then the use of asset purchases and other emergency measures. A response that helps contain one kind of failure can create a new question about allocation, prices or future risk. The evidence for the size and effect of any response needs a period, denominator and source. Read [managed-dollar crises](/after/03-debt-crises-and-the-managed-dollar-1982-1990/) and [the financial crisis and QE](/after/07-financial-crisis-and-the-age-of-qe-2007-2019/).

Required replacement, verbatim:

> The post-gold system relies on institutions with different jobs. Governments set legal obligations; central banks influence reserves and liquidity; commercial-bank lending creates deposits subject to capital, funding and borrower constraints; payment networks transmit instructions and settlement systems transfer balances. Debt and banking crises, cross-border funding pressures and other shocks have tested those arrangements and the rules applied to them. The chapters follow Latin American debt distress, emerging-market crises and the 2007–09 financial crash, then central-bank asset purchases and other emergency responses. Such responses can raise questions about who receives support, how prices change and what risks remain, but their sizes and effects require a defined period, measure and source. Read [managed-dollar crises](/after/03-debt-crises-and-the-managed-dollar-1982-1990/) and [the financial crisis and QE](/after/07-financial-crisis-and-the-age-of-qe-2007-2019/).

After Gold 03/07/10 approved summaries support bank-created deposits with constraints, distinct institutional mandates and QE as an asset-purchase response. The old line made cross-border flows themselves a rule failure and blurred payment messages with settled balances. The replacement is **editorially accepted** as conceptual synthesis; it makes no claim that a specific crisis response had a measured benefit, cost or causal effect. Dates and rescue arithmetic in the destination chapters remain evidence-review items.

## Gold reserves, denominators and recent shocks — **QUALIFY**

Current paragraph, verbatim:

> Gold did not disappear when currencies stopped promising gold redemption. States and households can still hold it as an asset; that is a different role from defining wages, taxes and debts by a weight of gold. A present-day reserve share or price should be read as a dated observation with a known denominator, not as evidence that the gold standard has returned. The same distinction matters in claims about dollar decline. Trade invoicing, reserves, debt and payment rails are separate measures, and a change in one does not automatically imply a change in the others. The gold volume's final chapters and the post-gold volume's recent chapter offer places to check those measures and their dates. This short reading does not supply a live number or a prediction; a future numeric edition should quote only accepted observations with their periods and sources. Read [gold's present role](/gold/09-gold-today-what-still-holds-its-value/) and [recent monetary shocks](/after/09-pandemic-inflation-and-weaponized-reserves-2020-2026/).

Required replacement, verbatim:

> Gold remained a monetary asset after currencies stopped promising conversion into a fixed weight of it. States and households may hold gold without defining wages, taxes and debts in gold. A reserve share needs a date, denominator and valuation method: when measured at market value, a higher gold price can raise the share without new purchases. That change alone does not show that a gold standard has returned or that the dollar has lost every monetary role. Trade invoicing, reserves, debt and payment arrangements are different measures; a movement in one does not determine the others. The gold volume and After Gold's recent chapter examine these roles and shocks, but any current quantity belongs with its period, measure and source. Read [gold's present role](/gold/09-gold-today-what-still-holds-its-value/) and [recent monetary shocks](/after/09-pandemic-inflation-and-weaponized-reserves-2020-2026/).

After Gold 09's approved summary supports the valuation-versus-reallocation distinction; After Gold 06/09/10 distinguish reserve immobilisation, loan commitments and currency roles. This paragraph is **editorially accepted** with the replacement wording, but it approves no 2025–26 reserve share, gold price, purchase total, sanctions outcome or forecast. A source link from a destination chapter is a place to review the measure, not a sign-off for every live assertion there.
