# 03 — How Bitcoin Is Actually Used: Global Adoption, 2009–2026
 
## The headline numbers
 
At the end of 2025 an estimated 741 million people owned some cryptocurrency and 365 million owned bitcoin — roughly one adult in fifteen worldwide. The US spot ETFs held 1.25 million coins for millions of brokerage accounts; some 190 public companies held about 1.25 million more. A comparable government-owned total cannot be established by summing labelled wallets: seizure, forfeiture, state-company ownership, fund investments and private declarations have different legal statuses. Bitcoin's market value in mid-September 2026 was about $1.55 trillion at a price near $77,000 — 5% of the world's gold and about 1.5% of its broad money. The question is not whether bitcoin has been adopted but *as what*.
 
## The dominant use: a savings and speculative asset
 
The strongest directly reviewed household measure is narrower than a worldwide Bitcoin-use estimate. In the Federal Reserve's 2025 survey, 10% of **U.S. adults** reported any cryptocurrency use, 9% reported buying or holding cryptocurrency as an investment, 2% reported buying something or making a payment with it, and 1% reported sending it to friends or family. Categories could overlap. These percentages do not give the fraction of Bitcoin owners who spend Bitcoin. [Federal Reserve, 2025 SHED, Banking, table 33](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-banking.htm). Exchange flows, on-chain transfer counts, mining fees and payment use measure different activities; the figures cited elsewhere in this chapter should not be combined into a single global payment share without compatible populations and methods.
 
The holding side has hardened. Coins on exchanges fell to their lowest share of supply since 2017 in 2026 — 1.3 to 2.7 million depending on the counting method, down from over 3.2 million in 2023–24 — as they moved into ETF custody, corporate treasuries and cold storage. About 14.5 million coins had not moved for five months or more in March 2026; a July 2026 tally put 11.2 million, 56% of supply, "outside active trade." Bitcoin behaves, on-chain, like gold in a vault.
 
## Who adopts it, and where
 
The geography of adoption is a map of monetary distrust and of youth. Chainalysis's 2025 Global Adoption Index, which weights on-chain activity by purchasing power, ranked India first, then the United States, Pakistan, Vietnam, Brazil, Nigeria, Indonesia, Ukraine, the Philippines and Russia; Ethiopia, Bangladesh, Türkiye, Venezuela and Argentina all sat in the top twenty. In raw dollars North America and Europe dominate — $2.2 trillion and $2.6 trillion received in the year to June 2025 — but relative to income the heaviest users are in countries with capital controls, inflation, large diasporas or weak banks. Sub-Saharan Africa received $205 billion between July 2024 and June 2025; transfers under $10,000 were a larger share of its on-chain value than in the rest of the world. [Chainalysis, 2025 Sub-Saharan Africa report](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/subsaharan-africa-crypto-adoption-2025/). Argentina received $94 billion and Venezuela $45 billion, overwhelmingly in stablecoins. TRM Labs' index for the first quarter of 2026 recorded $979 billion of global retail activity, down 11% on the year as prices fell, with the United States, South Korea, Russia, India, Brazil and Türkiye leading.

Nigeria shows why the activity measured matters. Chainalysis reports that Bitcoin accounted for **89% of fiat purchases of crypto on the centralized exchanges it covered** in Nigeria, while USDT accounted for 7%. Its 2025 geography report expressly excludes informal, business-to-business and over-the-counter transfers from that purchase comparison; the 89% is not a share of Nigerians, payments or all crypto flows. [Chainalysis, “Bitcoin holds dominance”](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/subsaharan-africa-crypto-adoption-2025/). Separately, the IMF's 2026 Nigeria report estimates that USDT and USDC made up **over 65% of Nigeria's crypto inflows in 2024**, using an on-chain cross-border-flow proxy available through January 2025. That is a different denominator and transaction channel, not a contradiction or proof that one asset dominated every use. [IMF Country Report 26/125, annex VII, pp. 64–65](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1ngaea2026001.pdf).
 
Three motives recur. In rich countries bitcoin is a portfolio asset — a high-volatility, high-return line item, increasingly bought through an ETF or a retirement plan (the August 2025 executive order opening US 401(k) plans to digital assets produced a Labor Department rule in March 2026). In middle-income countries with unstable currencies it is a flight asset, usually second to the stablecoin dollar. In poor and sanctioned countries it is a rail — a way to move value that the banking system will not carry, used by remittance senders, freelancers paid from abroad, opposition movements and, equally, by sanctioned governments and criminals.
 
## Institutional and sovereign adoption
 
The institutional channel was opened by the January 2024 ETF approvals and by accounting: from 2025 US companies could carry bitcoin at fair value rather than at cost-less-impairment, which removed the main objection to holding it. Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) is the extreme case — 845,050 coins by 31 August 2026, bought for $63.7 billion, financed by selling shares and preferred stock — and it spawned roughly a hundred imitators in 2025, from Japan's Metaplanet (43,000 coins) to Trump Media (12,000), several of which abandoned the strategy in 2026 when prices fell (file 12). Sovereign wealth funds appeared in ETF filings: Abu Dhabi's Mubadala held about $566 million of IBIT in March 2026; Norway's fund had indirect exposure of over 11,000 coins through its shareholdings. The Czech National Bank's $1 million test in November 2025 was the first purchase by a central bank. Larry Fink of BlackRock told Davos in January 2025 that sovereign funds were discussing 2–5% allocations.
 
The government record is heterogeneous. The US reserve order covers eligible finally forfeited Treasury bitcoin, not every seizure or forfeiture claim; the Prince Group announcement was a complaint, not a final order. UK prosecutors report recovery of about 61,000 bitcoin still subject to civil proceedings. China's PlusToken disposition is not verified by the cited wallet trackers, and Bhutan's documented state-linked mining does not make attributed wallet outflows proven sales. El Salvador's purchases, a sovereign fund's ETF investment and a central bank's test portfolio are also different forms of exposure. File 04 separates the cases and their legal status. [US reserve order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/establishment-of-the-strategic-bitcoin-reserve-and-united-states-digital-asset-stockpile/); [DOJ Prince Group announcement](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edny/pr/chairman-prince-group-indicted-operating-cambodian-forced-labor-scam-compounds-engaged); [UK CPS](https://www.cps.gov.uk/node/29684).
 
## The industry behind it
 
Mining is a roughly $12-billion-a-year industry with the economics of a commodity producer. In September 2026 the network's computing power was about 950 exahashes per second, down from a peak above one zettahash in autumn 2025, using an estimated 138 terawatt-hours a year — half a percent of world electricity, about what Poland uses — of which a Cambridge survey put 52% from sustainable sources. The United States hosts about 37% of the hashrate (75% of the hashrate that answers surveys), Russia 17%, China — where mining has been illegal since 2021 — about 12%. The industry's 2026 story is exit: with the average all-in cost of producing a coin (about $78,000–80,000) above the price for much of the year, listed miners signed over $70 billion of contracts to host artificial-intelligence computing instead, sold 32,000 coins in the first quarter, and unplugged about a fifth of their machines. Bitcoin now competes with AI for the power and capital that secure it (file 11).
 
Around mining sits the rest: exchanges (Binance, Coinbase, Bybit, OKX and a long tail; Coinbase lost $360 million in the second quarter of 2026 on $1.2 billion of revenue), custodians (Coinbase holds over 80% of ETF assets), market makers (Jump, Wintermute, Cumberland, Galaxy), stablecoin issuers (Tether earned $1.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026 on $115 billion of Treasuries and held 99,000 bitcoins of its own), and ETF sponsors collecting $350–400 million a year in fees. None of this existed in 2009; all of it is what a currency structure looks like when it is built by the private sector on top of an asset with no issuer.
 
## Payments: the small, real corner
 
Bitcoin is spent, in small amounts, in specific places. Merchant acceptance exists — Steak 'n Shake began taking it across US outlets in May 2025 and reported rising same-store sales without attributing them to bitcoin — and pockets of a bitcoin cash economy survive in El Salvador's El Zonte, in parts of Lugano, in Nigeria and among freelancers paid in it. The Lightning Network processed about 12 million transactions in late 2025, up 266% on the year, and carries over 15% of Coinbase's bitcoin withdrawals. But its public capacity — about 4,900 coins, $380 million — is less than a single day's ETF flow, most consumer wallets on it are custodial, and its fastest-growing use in 2026 is Tether's dollar. Bitcoin payments are a niche of a few billion dollars a year inside a stablecoin payment market of nine trillion.
 
## How bitcoin has behaved when it mattered
 
The crisis record cannot be compressed into one return or causal story. For a precisely dated example, the Grayscale Bitcoin Mini Trust ETF's June 2026 filing valued one bitcoin at $87,549.41 on 31 December 2025 and $58,745.18 on 30 June 2026, using its principal-market price at 4 p.m. New York time. That demonstrates a substantial decline over this six-month window; it is not a price path for every venue, an investor's after-fee return, or proof that a particular war caused the move. [Grayscale Bitcoin Mini Trust ETF, Form 10-Q, note 3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2015034/000201503426000008/btc-20260630.htm). Gold also fell sharply from a January 2026 intraday high to late June during the Middle East shock, according to the World Gold Council, but those two cited price conventions are not a matched return series. [WGC, *Gold Mid-Year Outlook 2026*, “A rollercoaster ride”](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-mid-year-outlook-2026). Earlier 2020–25 anecdotes, the late-August correlations and year-on-year rankings are withheld until the same source, dates, currency and cost treatment can be reproduced across assets.
 
These selected episodes show large bitcoin drawdowns and rebounds, but they are not a matched test of inflation protection or crisis performance. The timing of a shock, the investor's currency, the comparison asset and the holding window can change the result. An IMF staff study found stronger Bitcoin–equity co-movement after 2020 and a broader crypto factor sensitive to modeled U.S. monetary tightening in its 2018–23 sample. That is evidence against assuming an automatic market-risk hedge, not proof that bitcoin behaves identically in every later crisis. [IMF Working Paper 23/163, introduction, table 3 and conclusion](https://www.imf.org/en/-/media/files/publications/wp/2023/english/wpiea2023163-print-pdf.pdf). A reliable “debasement” or safe-haven claim needs dated, comparable return and drawdown series rather than a narrative selection of episodes.
 
## Key takeaways
 
Investment and trading are prominent uses of Bitcoin, but a defensible global split between holding, trading, payments and transfers has not yet been established here. The 2025 Federal Reserve survey provides U.S.-adult percentages for *cryptocurrency*, not Bitcoin-specific user shares: 9% reported investment use, 2% purchase/payment use and 1% sending to friends or family, with overlapping answers. Nigeria illustrates the measurement problem: Bitcoin's 89% share of covered centralized-exchange fiat purchases and dollar stablecoins' over-65% share of estimated 2024 crypto inflows describe different channels. Institutional holdings, regulated access and custodial products add other forms of exposure that should not be counted as unique owners without adjustment. Differences across countries and crises need their own dated, comparable evidence before this chapter can claim a universal adoption pattern or a reliable hedge function.
