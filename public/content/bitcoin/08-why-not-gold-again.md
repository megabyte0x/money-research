# 08 — Why Not Gold Again? Bitcoin Against the Metal It Imitates
 
## The question, stated precisely
 
If the world were to leave pure fiat and anchor money to something scarce again, why would the anchor be bitcoin rather than gold, which did the job for two and a half thousand years and which central banks are buying at the fastest rate since the 1960s? The question has two parts that are usually run together. The first is whether any *convertibility standard* — gold or bitcoin — will return. The evidence from files 07 and 08 of the After Gold set, and from file 07 here, is that it will not, for reasons that apply to both. The second is which of the two makes the better *reserve asset* in a world of floating fiat currencies, and there the answer in 2026 is unambiguous in one direction — central banks hold gold and not bitcoin — while the arguments for bitcoin are real and specific.
 
## First, what the world is actually doing: going back to gold as an asset, not as a standard
 
Central banks continue to hold and buy gold, but a rising *share* of reserves does not itself measure purchases. At end-2025 market prices the ECB put gold at 27% of official reserves **including gold**, above the euro (15%) and US Treasuries (22%) in that broad measure. The ECB attributed most of gold's rise in share to price valuation: using the end-2023 gold price puts gold and the euro near 16% each and Treasuries at 26%. The 2022 freeze of Russian assets is a plausible reason some reserve managers may prefer domestically held gold, but this aggregate comparison does not identify which portfolios changed for that reason. [ECB, June 2026, chart 7](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/other-publications/ire/html/ecb.ire202606.en.html). IMF COFER currency shares use a narrower foreign-exchange denominator that excludes gold. [IMF COFER](https://data.imf.org/Datasets/COFER).
 
None of this is a return to a gold standard. No central bank has proposed convertibility, no currency is defined as a weight of gold, and the reasons the standard ended — deflationary bias, the inability to finance wars and deficits, the 1930s, the Triffin dilemma — are as decisive as they were in 1971. What the world has done is choose gold as the *unfreezable reserve asset* of a floating-rate system. The relevant comparison for bitcoin is therefore not "bitcoin standard versus gold standard" but "bitcoin versus gold in the reserve portfolio of a state that keeps its own currency."
 
## The case for bitcoin over gold
 
**Verifiability.** A gold bar must be assayed; tungsten-filled fakes have reached bullion dealers. A bitcoin's authenticity is a mathematical fact anyone can check in seconds, and a reserve can be proven with a signature. The Fort Knox episode of 2025 is the contrast: the president and Elon Musk promised a public audit of the US gold reserve, the Treasury secretary then declared that "all the gold is present and accounted for," and the audit was quietly dropped. Gold reserves rest on institutional assurance; bitcoin reserves can be published.
 
**Settlement.** Final settlement of any amount of bitcoin between any two parties on earth takes about an hour. Gold settlement between central banks is a ledger entry at the Bank of England or the New York Fed — that is, trust in a custodian in London or New York — or a physical shipment. Germany took four years (2013–17) to repatriate 674 tonnes from New York and Paris.
 
**Supply.** Gold's above-ground stock grows 1.5–2% a year and rises when the price rises; bitcoin's is capped and its issuance falls by half every four years. For a debasement hedge this is the selling point; for a monetary standard, as file 07 explains, it is a defect, since gold's supply response is what dampens its volatility.
 
**Custody and confiscation.** Gold is only as unfreezable as the vault it sits in. Executive Order 6102 of 1933 compelled Americans to surrender private gold; the Bank of England has refused to release Venezuela's 31 tonnes since 2018, and the litigation over which Venezuelan government it recognises was still unresolved in 2026; gold held abroad by Russia would have been frozen with the rest. Self-custodied bitcoin cannot be frozen in transit or seized without the key, and a state can carry its whole reserve across a border as information. This is the property that gold offers only to states that keep it at home, and that bitcoin offers to anyone.
 
**Divisibility and portability.** A satoshi is a hundred-millionth of a coin; gold below a few grams is impractical. A billion dollars of bitcoin weighs nothing; a billion dollars of gold is about seven tonnes.
 
## The case for gold over bitcoin
 
**Track record.** Gold has held value through every regime since Lydia; bitcoin has seventeen years and has fallen 75–94% four times. A reserve asset's purpose is to be sold in a crisis, and gold has never gone to zero in one.
 
**Independence from infrastructure.** Gold needs no electricity, no internet, no keys, no developers and no consensus process. Bitcoin's security depends on a mining industry that in 2026 is leaving for artificial-intelligence hosting, on five people with the right to merge code, on a funding base dominated by one donor, and on the ability of a leaderless community to agree on a post-quantum upgrade before a cryptographically relevant computer exists. Google's March 2026 paper cut the estimated qubits needed to break bitcoin's signatures from millions to under half a million; estimates of coins in exposed-key addresses run from 1.7 million in the oldest outputs to 6.9 million — a third of supply — once every revealed public key is counted; the only remedies on the table (BIP-360 for new addresses, BIP-361 to freeze unmigrated coins after a deadline) require a contested decision that would confiscate Satoshi's 1.1 million coins and every other unmoved holding. Gold has no such cliff.
 
**No founder and no hidden whale.** About 1.1 million coins mined in 2009–10 have never moved. Their owner, if alive, could sell 5% of supply at will. Gold has no Satoshi.
 
**Lower volatility.** Gold's realised volatility is 9–15%; bitcoin's 23–45%. In the Iran war both were sold in the first week for dollar liquidity, but gold fell 25% from its record and bitcoin 53% from its peak over the cycle; over the year to September 2026 gold was up 30% and bitcoin down 33%. A reserve manager who must show a portfolio to a finance minister cannot yet hold much of the second.
 
**Neutral settlement between adversaries.** Between 2022 and 2026 gold moved physically between Russia, China, Iran, Türkiye and the Gulf under sanctions, because a bar in a vault in Shanghai is beyond OFAC's reach. Bitcoin is not obviously more neutral: its hashrate is 37% American, its ETFs are American, its custodians and exchanges screen for sanctions, and the United States holds 328,000 coins and licenses the stablecoin that prices it. Iran's central bank turned to crypto for imports in September 2026 as a rail, not as a reserve.
 
**Regulatory status.** Under Basel, allocated physical gold carries a 0% credit-risk weight and counts among the assets a bank can hold against its own currency; bitcoin carries 1,250% and a 1–2% exposure cap. The official sector has written, in capital rules, that gold is reserve-grade and bitcoin is not.
 
## Why a restored standard would not be gold — and would not be bitcoin either
 
The gold standard ended for four reasons, and they are worth listing because each applies to a bitcoin standard with equal or greater force. Deflationary bias and sticky wages: mass democracies, as Eichengreen showed, would not subordinate employment to a metal, and would not subordinate it to a hash function. War and emergency finance: every belligerent suspended convertibility in 1914 and 1939, and a state at war in 2026 — Russia, Ukraine, Israel, Iran, the United States in the Gulf — finances it by borrowing in its own currency, which a bitcoin standard forbids. The 1930s: the countries that left gold first recovered first, and no finance minister has forgotten it. The Triffin dilemma: any exchange standard in which a centre country supplies reserves must eventually issue more claims than it holds, and a bitcoin-exchange standard would break exactly as 1931 and 1971 did.
 
Bitcoin then adds three problems gold never had: a supply that cannot respond to demand at all (White's vertical supply curve); protocol and key risk (quantum, governance, the 2010 overflow bug that briefly created 184 billion coins); and, per the Minneapolis Fed's 2024 paper, an explicit modelled incentive for deficit-running states to tax or prohibit it. Weber's 2016 conclusion stands: the obstacle to a bitcoin standard "is not technical but political," because policymakers "would feel constrained by the limited monetary flexibility" it permits — which is precisely why they left gold.
 
So the honest answer to "why not gold again?" is that a *standard* will not return in either form, and that a *reserve asset* is returning in gold's form because gold satisfies the requirements a reserve manager is legally and professionally bound by — liquidity in a crisis, low volatility, no counterparty, no technical failure mode, a capital rule that recognises it — and bitcoin does not yet satisfy any of them. What bitcoin offers that gold does not — verifiability, teleportability, a fixed cap, and confiscation resistance that does not depend on the vault being at home — is real and is the reason the Czech central bank, Texas, Abu Dhabi's funds and BlackRock's clients hold some. The question the next decade will answer is whether those properties are worth the volatility, the infrastructure dependence and the political exposure, and the answer will be given in allocation percentages, not in standards.
 
## Key takeaways
 
Gold remains an established official reserve asset; Bitcoin's proposed reserve role requires separate analysis of law, liquidity, volatility, custody and operational risk. At end-2025 prices, gold's share exceeded the euro's and US Treasuries' in the ECB's broad reserve measure, largely through valuation effects. This does not imply the dollar lost the same share inside IMF COFER, which excludes gold, nor does it prove that the 2022 reserve freeze caused each gold purchase. Neither a renewed gold convertibility standard nor a Bitcoin standard follows automatically from these holdings.
