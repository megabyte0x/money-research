# 15 — Glossary
 
**51% attack** — A majority-hashpower attack on transaction history: an attacker may build a competing valid chain to reverse recent confirmations or exclude selected transactions from its own blocks. Hashpower cannot make independently validating nodes accept blocks that violate their chosen consensus rules. The cost and feasibility of buying, renting or coercing hashpower depend on a dated market and threat model. [Bitcoin Developer Guide, “Proof Of Work”](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html#proof-of-work).
 
**ASIC** — Application-specific integrated circuit; a purpose-built chip used for Bitcoin mining. A manufacturer's market share needs a dated shipment, installed-fleet or hashrate denominator.
 
**Bitcoin standard** — A term used for different arrangements: Bitcoin as base money and unit of account in an economy; a specified national currency redeemable in Bitcoin; or Bitcoin held as a floating reserve or private asset beside another unit. Supply, credit, redemption and elasticity questions depend on which arrangement is meant (file 07).
 
**Bitcoinisation** — A proposed shift toward Bitcoin as a country's widely used money or unit of account. El Salvador's 2021 mandatory-acceptance law was a narrower experiment alongside the U.S. dollar, not full Bitcoinisation; its 2025 amendment made private acceptance voluntary while retaining “curso legal” wording.
 
**BIP** — Bitcoin Improvement Proposal: a published specification or discussion, not automatically an activated consensus rule. BIP-360 is a draft soft-fork specification and BIP-361 a draft informational migration proposal; neither sets a binding Bitcoin quantum deadline. [BIP-360](https://bips.dev/360/); [BIP-361](https://bips.dev/361/).
 
**Block subsidy** — Newly issuable coins in a valid block reward, separate from transaction fees. Under the present 210,000-block halving rule, the maximum subsidy is 3.125 BTC for heights 840,000–1,049,999 and 1.5625 BTC from height 1,050,000. A subsidy halving does not by itself halve total miner income or its dollar value. [Bitcoin Developer Guide, “Block Chain”](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html); [Bitcoin protocol reference, “Serialized Blocks”](https://developer.bitcoin.org/reference/block_chain.html#serialized-blocks).
 
**Cash creation/redemption** — An ETF process in which authorised participants exchange cash rather than bitcoin for fund shares. Shares represent an interest in the trust's net assets, not one coin each; the trust's filed holdings and liabilities, rather than the creation method alone, establish its asset position. [iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1980994/000143774926026004/bit20260630c_10q.htm).
 
**Chivo** — El Salvador's government-backed Bitcoin wallet launched during the 2021 legal-tender experiment. Its user activity, incentive take-up and later ownership/operation require dated program and transaction records; the earlier bonus, user-share and 2026 privatisation claims are withheld here.
 
**CLARITY Act** — U.S. House-passed digital-asset market-structure bill, H.R. 3633. A Senate cloture step on the *motion to proceed* was scheduled to ripen on 15 September 2026 in a pre-vote Senate record; that record does not establish a vote outcome, passage or enactment. [Senate floor activity, 8 August 2026](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/floor_activity/08_07_2026_Senate_Floor.htm); [Senate Daily Press, 10 September schedule](https://www.dailypress.senate.gov/thursday-september-10-2026/).
 
**Cryptoization** — The IMF's term in its October 2021 financial-stability report for crypto-asset and currency substitution in emerging-market and developing economies. The report discusses possible capital-flow, monetary and control risks; it does not establish that every instance reinforces dollarisation or evades controls. [IMF, *Global Financial Stability Report*, October 2021, ch. 2](https://www.imf.org/-/media/Files/Publications/GFSR/2021/October/English/ch2.ashx).
 
**Custodian** — A firm that safeguards keys or assets on behalf of another legal owner, such as an ETF trust. Assets it controls for clients are not necessarily its own beneficial holdings; custody concentration and ownership concentration are different measures.
 
**Debasement trade** — A market label for positioning in assets such as gold or Bitcoin against perceived currency-value erosion. Whether either asset hedges a specified currency shock, or has higher beta, requires a defined period and matched returns.
 
**Debt-deflation** — Irving Fisher's 1933 mechanism: falling prices raise the real burden of fixed nominal debts and may induce distress sales that put further pressure on prices. Applying it to a Bitcoin-denominated economy depends on its actual debt contracts, credit and monetary arrangement.
 
**Difficulty adjustment** — A protocol recalculation every 2,016 blocks, targeted at roughly two weeks for that block interval if hashrate is unchanged. Blocks can arrive faster or slower before and after an adjustment; a sudden miner loss need not cause exactly two weeks of delay. [Bitcoin Developer Guide, “Proof Of Work”](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html#proof-of-work).
 
**Digital Asset Stockpile** — A separate U.S. federal executive-order account for eligible non-Bitcoin digital assets in Treasury possession, distinguished from the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve. A seizure or claim is not itself a verified present inventory. [White House order, §§2–3](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/establishment-of-the-strategic-bitcoin-reserve-and-united-states-digital-asset-stockpile/).
 
**Elasticity** — The capacity of a specified monetary aggregate to respond to demand. Bitcoin's base issuance schedule is not discretionary, but Bitcoin-denominated bank claims and credit could change. Emergency lending would depend on whether a redeemable issuer exists and how much redemption risk it can bear; a Bitcoin-only system would lack such an issuer. [Weber, 2016, §3](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/swp2016-14.pdf).
 
**Exposed public key** — A spending public key already visible in an unspent output or revealed through address reuse or a prior spend. Such exposure could enable a long-exposure attack if a sufficiently capable quantum computer exists; counts depend on output-type and reuse assumptions, not merely the number of addresses. [BIP-360, threat model](https://bips.dev/360/).
 
**Fair-value accounting** — Under U.S. FASB ASU 2023-08, an asset that meets the standard's crypto-asset criteria is measured at fair value each reporting period, with changes in net income. The update applies for fiscal years beginning after 15 December 2024, with early adoption permitted; it does not by itself establish why corporate treasuries adopted Bitcoin. [FASB ASU 2023-08](https://storage.fasb.org/ASU%202023-08.pdf).
 
**GENIUS Act** — U.S. Public Law 119-27, approved 18 July 2025, establishes a federal framework for *payment stablecoins* and permitted issuers, rather than every token described as a stablecoin. Section 4(a)(1) requires permitted issuers to maintain identifiable reserves on at least a one-to-one basis and lists deposits, short-dated Treasuries, specified repurchase agreements, government money-market funds and other allowed assets; it does not limit reserves to cash and Treasury bills. Sections 4(a)(3) and 4(a)(10) distinguish monthly reserve-report examination from a threshold-based annual financial-statement audit. Section 7(e) excludes federal deposit/share insurance for payment stablecoins. Section 20 makes the Act effective on the earlier of 18 months after enactment or 120 days after primary federal regulators issue final implementing rules; enactment alone did not license all existing issuers. [Enacted GENIUS Act, §§2–4, 7(e), 20](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/html/PLAW-119publ27.htm).
 
**Gresham's Law / Thiers' Law** — Heuristics about currency choice under different monetary rules: an enforced exchange ratio may encourage spending the overvalued money and holding the undervalued one (Gresham); voluntary choice amid a failing currency can favour the stronger money (Thiers). Neither alone establishes why people did or did not use Bitcoin in El Salvador.
 
**Halving** — The programmed cut in the maximum Bitcoin block subsidy every 210,000 blocks. The next scheduled boundary under the present rule is height 1,050,000; its calendar date and effects on fees, price and hashrate are not fixed by the rule. [Bitcoin protocol reference, “Serialized Blocks”](https://developer.bitcoin.org/reference/block_chain.html#serialized-blocks).
 
**Hashrate** — An estimate of hashing attempts per second by miners, commonly expressed in exahashes (10¹⁸) or zettahashes (10²¹) per second. Whole-network hashrate is inferred from block production and difficulty rather than counted directly; a current value needs its date, averaging window and method.
 
**Hyperbitcoinisation** — A proposed rapid shift toward Bitcoin as widely used money. It is a scenario, not a measured adoption law; its trigger and pace must be specified by the person using the term.
 
**IBIT** — iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF, sponsored by a BlackRock affiliate. The trust reports bitcoin as its asset, a custodian holds it on the trust's behalf, and shareholders hold beneficial interests in the trust's net assets. These are not additive coin holdings. [SEC-filed Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1980994/000143774926026004/bit20260630c_10q.htm).
 
**Knots** — An alternative Bitcoin full-node software distribution with policy settings that may differ from Bitcoin Core. Software policy, consensus validity and observed node-installation share are different questions.
 
**Legal tender** — A legal status whose consequences depend on the jurisdiction; acceptance for new purchases, debt discharge, tax payment and pricing should be checked separately. El Salvador's 2025 amendment kept the phrase “curso legal” but made private Bitcoin acceptance voluntary and repealed the prior tax-payment authorization. The IMF describes this as removing the essential features of legal tender. [Decree 199](https://www.jurisprudencia.gob.sv/DocumentosBoveda/R/2/2020-2029/2021/06/1080B6.HTML?embedded=true); [IMF Country Report 25/58, para. 27 n.18](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1slvea2025001-print-pdf.pdf).
 
**Lightning Network** — A Bitcoin payment-channel network in which participants commit funds to channels and can route payments through peers. Public channel capacity is a visible liquidity measure, not a measure of payment volume or custodial-app use; the earlier dated capacity and issuer-integration claims require separate records.
 
**Lost coins** — Outputs whose spending keys are irrecoverable, a status generally not observable from the chain alone. Long inactivity, including coins attributed to early miners, is not proof of loss. Provably unspendable outputs are a narrower, separately measurable category. [Bitcoin Core, `gettxoutsetinfo`](https://bitcoincore.org/en/doc/29.0.0/rpc/blockchain/gettxoutsetinfo/).
 
**MiCA** — The EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation. Titles III–IV apply from 30 June 2024 and most provisions from 30 December 2024; Article 143 permits qualifying existing crypto-asset service providers to continue until 1 July 2026 or an earlier authorisation decision, while Member States may shorten or omit that transition. These dates do not verify a named provider's present authorisation. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, arts. 143 and 149](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32023R1114).
 
**mNAV** — A treasury-company valuation ratio comparing a specified measure of the company's market value with a specified measure of its Bitcoin holdings' value. Debt, cash, other assets and the share-count convention affect the ratio; the earlier Strategy levels and causal financing conclusion need matched dated filings and prices.
 
**Node** — A computer participating in the Bitcoin network. A fully validating node checks received blocks and transactions against its chosen rules; miners propose blocks, while network adoption of rules depends on participants' software and incentives. “Reachable node” counts cover an observable subset and need a date and measurement method. [Bitcoin Developer Guide, “Introduction” and “Consensus Rule Changes”](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html).
 
**OP_RETURN** — A transaction output type that carries arbitrary data; the subject of the 2025–26 Core-versus-Knots dispute.
 
**Paper bitcoin** — A shorthand for Bitcoin-denominated contractual or custodial claims whose amount, redemption terms or issuer holdings differ from self-custodied coins. An uncovered claim requires evidence of the issuer's assets and liabilities; an exchange failure or derivative position is not automatically proof of a specified reserve shortfall.
 
**Perpetual futures** — Derivatives without a fixed expiry, with funding payments used to keep a contract price near a reference market. Settlement asset, offered leverage, price-discovery share and liquidation totals vary by venue and date; the former blanket figures are withheld.
 
**Proof of reserves** — A report or demonstration about specified assets at a point in time, with scope set by its procedures. It is not automatically an audit and may fail to test liabilities, rights to assets or borrowed funds; read its actual scope before inferring solvency. [PCAOB staff investor advisory](https://pcaobus.org/resources/information-for-investors/investor-advisories/investor-advisory-exercise-caution-with-third-party-verification-proof-of-reserve-reports).
 
**Proof of work** — Bitcoin's method of requiring miners to find a block-header hash below a target before proposing a block. It raises the work needed to rewrite transaction history; security also depends on validation and network behavior, while energy use needs a separate measured estimate. [Bitcoin Developer Guide, “Proof Of Work”](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html#proof-of-work).
 
**Satoshi (sat)** — One hundred-millionth of a bitcoin, a smaller denomination of the asset. Its existence does not establish widespread use as an economic unit of account.
 
**Security budget** — A shorthand for miner revenue from subsidy plus transaction fees over a stated period, usually expressed in BTC or converted to a fiat value at a dated exchange rate. It is not itself the cost of acquiring, operating or coercing attack hashpower. [Bitcoin Developer Guide, “Block Chain”](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html); [Budish, NBER Working Paper 24717](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24717).
 
**SegWit / Taproot** — Named Bitcoin consensus-rule upgrades activated in 2017 and 2021. They are examples of rule changes, not an exhaustive count of every change since then.
 
**Stablecoin** — A token designed to maintain a target value relative to a currency or another reference asset. Asset-backed and algorithmic designs use different mechanisms; neither the label nor the target price alone establishes a holder's redemption right, reserve composition or deposit-insurance protection. Dollar-denominated stablecoins can serve as trading and settlement instruments while importing the dollar as their pricing reference. [BIS, *The future monetary system*, ch. III, “The promise and pitfalls of crypto”](https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2022/future-monetary-system).
 
**Strategic Bitcoin Reserve** — Established by US executive order on 6 March 2025 for eligible Treasury-held bitcoin that has been finally forfeited and is not needed for other statutory purposes. A seizure or pending forfeiture complaint is not automatically part of the reserve; the order does not publish a reconciled balance. [White House executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/establishment-of-the-strategic-bitcoin-reserve-and-united-states-digital-asset-stockpile/).
 
**Stratum V2** — A mining-pool protocol whose Job Declaration component can allow miner-side template/transaction selection under a compatible implementation; the basic Mining Protocol can also operate without that component. Support announcements do not establish actual network deployment or the share of blocks built with miner-selected templates. [Stratum V2 protocol overview](https://stratumprotocol.org/specification/03-protocol-overview/).
 
**Tail emission** — A proposed perpetual small block subsidy to fund security after the halvings; would abandon the 21 million cap.
 
**Template proxying** — A pool or miner using a block template supplied by another operator. It differs from who contributes hashpower, who announces the block and who validates it; a share of templates needs block-level attribution rules and a dated sample.
 
**Treasury company** — A listed company for which holding Bitcoin is a material corporate financing or investment strategy. A company's coins, liabilities and corporate classification must be taken from dated filings; the former Strategy coin count and imitator total are withheld from this timeless definition.
 
**Triffin dilemma** — A tension in the Bretton Woods dollar–gold arrangement: supplying internationally used dollar claims while maintaining a fixed gold-redemption promise could strain confidence in that promise. A Bitcoin-backed national claim would face a comparable tension only if its design combined a central issuer, international reserve use and a specified Bitcoin-redemption promise; excess issuance and a run are possible outcomes, not universal laws of every exchange standard. See file 07, “Redeemable claims.”
 
**Unit of account** — The unit in which prices, wages, debts and other obligations are stated. Payment in Bitcoin at a price translated from dollars is different from native Bitcoin denomination. The extent of native use requires evidence about actual contracts and prices, not a universal “never” claim.
 
**UTXO** — Unspent transaction output; bitcoin's ledger records coins as outputs, each traceable to the block that created it.
 
**Wrench attack** — Physical coercion intended to obtain a holder's keys or force a transfer. Incident counts depend on reporting and verification criteria; the former 2025 total and France frequency are withheld.
