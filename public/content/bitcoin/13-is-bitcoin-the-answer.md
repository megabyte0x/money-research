# 13 — Is Bitcoin the True Solution to Money After Gold?
 
## The question the whole project has been building toward
 
The first directory asked why gold became money and why the world stopped using it; the second asked how a world with no anchor organised money for fifty-five years. This volume has asked whether bitcoin — the fiat era's deliberate attempt to rebuild gold in software — is the answer to the problem 1971 left open: what backs money when nothing does. The question has to be split into three, because "bitcoin as the solution" means three different things (file 07), and the evidence answers each differently.
 
## Is bitcoin a solution to the store-of-value problem fiat created?
 
Yes, with a qualification about time. Fiat money's defect as a store of value is that its supply is a political decision: US broad money grew $7.7 trillion in six years, the major central banks expanded their balance sheets by some $20 trillion between 2008 and 2022 by buying assets with new money, and every fiat currency has lost most of its purchasing power since 1971. Bitcoin's supply is a public fact. Over any period of five years or more it has outperformed every currency and every asset class — 25% a year compounded over the five years to end-2025, 70% over ten — and it has done so while the institutions that were supposed to be its rivals absorbed it: ETFs, pensions, corporate treasuries, sovereign funds, a US executive order. Three hundred and sixty-five million people hold it; the largest asset manager in the world calls it "digital gold"; the Czech central bank bought some. The property that makes it work is the one gold has and fiat lacks: no one can make more, and the holder does not need anyone's permission. That is a genuine solution to a genuine problem.
 
The qualification is the one every crisis of the last six years has taught: bitcoin stores value across a cycle, not through a crash. It fell 50% in two days in March 2020, 64% in 2022, 53% between October 2025 and June 2026; in the Iran war it was sold with everything else on the first day and outperformed gold only after the liquidity panic had passed; over the year to September 2026 gold rose 30% and bitcoin fell 33%. It is a hedge against *debasement* — the slow loss of a currency's value — and not a hedge against *crisis*, when the world wants dollars. Gold is both, which is why central banks hold gold.
 
## Is bitcoin a solution to the money problem — a unit of account and medium of exchange?
 
No, and not because of anything that can be fixed. The evidence of files 02–04 is uniform: under 2% of users spend it, the base layer clears seven transactions a second, its scaling layer is shrinking and carrying dollars, the one country that made it legal tender saw usage fall to 8% of the population and repealed the law, and nothing on earth is priced in it. The reasons are structural (file 11): a vertical supply curve that puts every change in demand into the price, so that volatility stays at three to five times gold's; a fixed base that cannot expand in a panic, so that any credit system built on it recreates the bank runs of 1907 (file 12); a security budget that halves every four years; and a tax and legal regime that makes fiat the unit of account by law. A bitcoin *standard* in the strict sense — bitcoin as the denominator of wages, debts and taxes — would reproduce the classical gold standard's deflation, fixed exchange rates and loss of monetary policy (file 07), and every reason the world abandoned that standard applies to bitcoin with more force. Warren Weber's conclusion for the Bank of Canada in 2016 has not been overtaken: the obstacle "is not technical but political," because no democracy that runs deficits will subordinate employment to a fixed supply, and the Minneapolis Fed has since shown formally why such a state would rather tax or ban it.
 
## Is bitcoin a solution to the problem of trust in the institutions that issue money?
 
Partly, and paradoxically. The deepest lesson of the After Gold volume was that fiat money is backed by trust in institutions — independent central banks, treaties, courts, the rule of law — and that the 2022 freezing of Russia's reserves shattered that trust for much of the world and sent central banks to gold. Bitcoin offers the same escape as gold, in a form that can be verified by anyone, carried as information and settled in an hour, and for individuals in Nigeria, Argentina, Lebanon or Canada in February 2022 it has already delivered it. But for states, the escape turns out to run through American infrastructure: 37% of the hashrate, the ETFs, the custodians, the pricing stablecoin and the regulatory perimeter are under US jurisdiction, and the US holds 328,000 coins. The structure built around bitcoin — the dollar stablecoin above all — has made the dollar *more* entrenched, not less: the money invented to escape the state is priced, settled and increasingly paid in the state's currency, and the state has licensed that arrangement. A state that distrusts Washington buys gold; a state that trusts it can hold bitcoin as a small line in the same portfolio.
 
## So what is bitcoin, in the terms of this project?
 
In the language of the first directory: bitcoin is a *synthetic commodity money* — absolutely scarce, with no industrial use, like gold without the jewellery — that has completed the first two rungs of monetisation (collectible, store of value) in seventeen years and stalled at the third. In the language of the second: it is the fiat era's gold ETF for people who do not trust the vault — a reserve asset that lives inside the dollar system as a hedge against the dollar. It is not, on any current evidence, the replacement for fiat money after the gold standard. It is the first credible non-state reserve asset created since gold, and the structure that has grown around it is the strongest evidence yet that the dollar's network can absorb even the money built to escape it.
 
## What would have to change
 
For bitcoin to move from reserve asset to money, in order of likelihood: a decade of volatility under 10% a year, which its design makes improbable; wages, debts and taxes denominated in it, which requires the volatility first; a credit system with a lender of last resort in bitcoin, which requires either states holding it in size or fractional-reserve bitcoin banking with all its history; a resolution of the security-budget and quantum questions without breaking the cap or the community; and a fiat crisis severe enough to run Thiers' Law — people fleeing the dollar into something scarce — in which bitcoin, on its record, would be sold first and bought afterwards, and in which gold would be the first destination. For bitcoin to become a *reserve asset of major states*, the list is shorter and closer: a US statutory reserve and a first public purchase; one or two central banks at 1% with statutory cover; a Basel risk weight that recognises it; and a quantum migration completed without a schism. Those are the things to watch in the next five years, and none of them makes it the unit of account.
 
## A note toward the next question
 
The project's end goal is to judge the value proposition of bitcoin and of Zcash as a unit of economic value. This volume's verdict on bitcoin sets the criteria: a candidate must offer what gold offers (scarcity, no issuer, no counterparty) plus what gold lacks (verifiability, portability, settlement), and must then confront the four structural problems — volatility, inelasticity, security budget, governance — that stop bitcoin at the store-of-value rung. Zcash adds one property bitcoin does not have, transactional privacy, which speaks directly to the censorship-resistance and neutrality questions in files 02 and 08 — and which every regulator in file 05 treats as the property most hostile to the AML perimeter. It should be judged against the same ladder and the same defects, with that trade-off at the centre.
 
## Key takeaways
 
Bitcoin is a solution to one of the three problems fiat left open and not to the other two. As a store of value it works: a supply no one can inflate, a holder no one can exclude, and returns that beat every currency over any five-year period — though it is a hedge against slow debasement, sold first in every fast crisis, and gold remains the asset that is both. As money it does not work and cannot be made to: volatility, inelasticity, the security-budget cliff and fiat-denominated tax law keep it a reserve asset, and a bitcoin standard would reproduce every defect that ended the gold standard plus some gold never had. As an escape from institutional trust it works for individuals and, paradoxically, has bound states more tightly to the dollar, whose stablecoins, custodians, ETFs and regulators now form bitcoin's own plumbing. Bitcoin is the first non-state reserve asset since gold and the fiat era's most successful synthetic commodity; it is not the replacement for fiat money, and nothing in its design or its record suggests it will become one without a collapse of fiat that would send the world to gold first.
