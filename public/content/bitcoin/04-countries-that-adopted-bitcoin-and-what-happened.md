# 04 — Countries That Adopted Bitcoin, in What Form, and What Happened
 
## Four forms of adoption
 
"Adoption" by a state can mean several different things: requiring or permitting Bitcoin payments, holding it as a public asset, mining it, or regulating private services built around it. A statutory legal-tender label does not by itself establish mandatory acceptance, tax-payment rights, or widespread use. El Salvador's 2021 law required acceptance; its 2025 amendment made private acceptance voluntary while retaining “curso legal” wording, which the IMF says no longer has the essential features of legal tender. The Central African Republic's separate experiment also ended, but its legal path was different. [El Salvador, Decree 199](https://www.jurisprudencia.gob.sv/DocumentosBoveda/R/2/2020-2029/2021/06/1080B6.HTML?embedded=true); [IMF Country Report 25/58, para. 27 n.18](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1slvea2025001-print-pdf.pdf). Public holdings, mining and regulation are distinct from making Bitcoin a national unit of account.
 
## El Salvador: the only full experiment
 
**What was done.** On 8–9 June 2021 the Legislative Assembly passed the Bitcoin Law by 62 votes of 84; it took effect on 7 September 2021. Bitcoin became legal tender alongside the dollar, which had been El Salvador's only currency since 2001. Every merchant was required to accept it, taxes could be paid in it, and the state launched the Chivo wallet with a $30 sign-up bonus (about 1% of average annual income) and a $150 million trust to convert bitcoin to dollars on demand. The stated aims were to cut the cost of remittances (23–24% of GDP, 98% from the United States), to bank a population of which only about a third had accounts, and to attract investment and tourism. Announced alongside it were a $1 billion "Volcano Bond," a tax-free Bitcoin City at the foot of the Conchagua volcano, and geothermal mining.
 
**What happened to usage.** The independent evidence is unusually good because economists surveyed 1,800 households in 2022 (the NBER study by Alvarez, Argente and Van Patten). About half of households downloaded Chivo; over 60% made no transaction after spending the bonus; 20% of businesses accepted bitcoin; about 5% of sales through Chivo were in bitcoin, and 88% of firms receiving it converted immediately to dollars. Users were young, educated, male and already banked. The Central American University's annual survey then tracked decline: 25.7% of Salvadorans said they had used bitcoin for a purchase in 2021, 21% in 2022, 12% in 2023 and 8.1% in 2024. Remittances through crypto wallets were 1.9% of the total in the first months, and 0.7% in the first half of 2026. Prices in El Salvador remained in dollars throughout; the unit of account never moved.
 
**What it cost and what it earned.** The state spent about $85 million of the trust in the first months, up to $75 million on Chivo bonuses, and — by the IMF's reckoning — about 0.7% of GDP on the whole initiative. It bought roughly 2,400 coins in 2021–22 at prices "well above" later levels and was underwater by half in 2022. It then kept buying one coin a day, and, after prices recovered, sat on a paper gain of $386 million by May 2025. In September 2026 the government's tracker showed 7,762 coins, worth about $600 million.
 
**The IMF programme and the 2025 reform.** After the IMF urged El Salvador to narrow Bitcoin's official role, the Legislative Assembly adopted **Decree 199 on 29 January 2025**, published on 30 January and effective 90 days after publication. It *amended* rather than repealed the Bitcoin Law. The revised article 1 still calls Bitcoin “curso legal” (legal tender/legal currency in the statute's wording), but explicitly makes acceptance voluntary for private persons and wholly private entities. Revised article 7 limits who may accept it; revised article 3 allows prices to be *converted* into Bitcoin; article 7 of the decree repeals three earlier provisions, including the authorization for tax payment in Bitcoin. State monetary obligations must be paid in the currencies in which they were contracted. Thus neither “all Bitcoin legal-tender language was repealed in January” nor “merchants still had to accept it” describes the text. The IMF's February 2025 staff report describes the reform as removing the **essential features** of legal tender and requiring taxes in U.S. dollars, while the domestic statute retains its label. [El Salvador, Decree 199, arts. 1–8](https://www.jurisprudencia.gob.sv/DocumentosBoveda/R/2/2020-2029/2021/06/1080B6.HTML?embedded=true); [IMF Country Report 25/58, para. 27 n.18](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1slvea2025001-print-pdf.pdf). Programme commitments about Chivo and public holdings are separate from the text of Decree 199 and require their own implementation checks.
 
**The economic change, and what caused it.** El Salvador's economy improved markedly: cumulative growth of 19% in 2021–23 against about 2% a year before, 3.9% growth in 2025 and 4.5% projected for 2026; homicides down from 51 per 100,000 in 2018 to 1.3 in 2025; tourism up; and a chain of credit-rating upgrades from Caa3/CCC+ in 2022 to B/B-/B3 by early 2025. Every rating agency and the IMF attribute this to the security crackdown, fiscal consolidation, debt buybacks and pension reform — not to bitcoin, which the IMF describes as "ring-fenced." Bitcoin's real contributions were a paper gain on the reserve, Tether's relocation of its headquarters to El Salvador in January 2025 after obtaining a licence, an August 2025 investment-banking law allowing licensed "bitcoin banks," and a global brand. The Volcano Bond was never issued; Bitcoin City has no buildings; state geothermal mining exists only as a private pool. The Economist called the project a failure in 2025 and President Bukele conceded in 2024 that it had not delivered the results he hoped for. The best summary is that El Salvador ran the world's only test of bitcoin as legal tender in a dollarised economy with a popular government fully behind it, and the population declined to use it.
 
## Central African Republic: the second experiment, in twelve months
 
On 21 April 2022 the CAR — per-capita income about $800, internet access about 10%, a member of the CFA franc zone — passed Law 22.004 making crypto legal tender alongside the CFA franc. Within a week the regional central bank BEAC and the CEMAC commission objected that the law violated the monetary union treaty giving BEAC sole authority over legal tender; the regional banking regulator barred banks from any crypto business; and the Constitutional Court struck down the provisions selling citizenship and land for the state's "Sango" token. Sango, launched in July 2022 to raise $2.5 billion (equal to the country's GDP — $1 billion in coin sales plus citizenship at $60,000 and land at $10,000), had sold under $2 million by January 2023. On 6 April 2023 Law 23.005 removed legal-tender status. The IMF's assessment was that the scheme was "too complex" and could raise systemic risk; a research institute described it as state capture rather than inclusion. In February 2025 the president launched a memecoin, $CAR, which reached a $894 million market value and lost 97% within a day. No measurable economic effect was recorded at any point.
 
## The United States: a reserve with a legal-status test
 
Federal law enforcement has seized bitcoin in cases including Silk Road and the Bitfinex hack. The 6 March 2025 executive order capitalises the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve with bitcoin held by the Treasury that has been **finally forfeited** and is not needed for other statutory purposes; it also directs an inventory and establishes a separate stockpile for other forfeited digital assets. Seizure, a forfeiture claim, final forfeiture and transfer into the reserve are different events. In October 2025 the Justice Department filed a **civil forfeiture complaint** concerning approximately 127,271 bitcoin linked to the Prince Group case. That announcement was not a final forfeiture judgment, so those coins cannot be added to a verified reserve balance on its authority. Public wallet trackers do not supply a reconciled Treasury inventory by legal status, beneficial ownership and date; this article therefore withholds a US total instead of repeating a 328,000-coin figure. Federal purchases have not been established by the cited record, while purchase bills remain proposals. [White House executive order, §§ 3–4](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/establishment-of-the-strategic-bitcoin-reserve-and-united-states-digital-asset-stockpile/); [DOJ Prince Group announcement](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edny/pr/chairman-prince-group-indicted-operating-cambodian-forced-labor-scam-compounds-engaged).
 
The states moved faster than Washington. New Hampshire (May 2025) allows up to 5% of public funds in digital assets with a market value over $500 billion, which means bitcoin; Arizona (May 2025) created a reserve funded only by unclaimed property and seizures after the governor vetoed a purchase bill; Texas (June 2025) appropriated $10 million, bought about $5 million of the BlackRock ETF in November 2025 at about $87,000 — the first state purchase — and in mid-2026 tendered to move the whole allocation into direct custody. Bills in Florida, Wyoming, Montana, North Dakota and Pennsylvania failed. The federal regime around the asset advanced further than the reserve: the GENIUS Act of July 2025 licensed dollar stablecoins, the August 2025 executive order opened 401(k) plans, and the CLARITY market-structure bill faced a Senate cloture vote on 15 September 2026.
 
## Bhutan: state-linked mining, uncertain disposition
 
Bhutan's state-owned Druk Holding and Investments announced a hydropower-backed bitcoin-mining partnership in 2023. State-linked mining is documented, but a labelled wallet is not a complete audited statement of Druk's holdings. Transfers from attributed wallets may be sales, transfers to custodians, collateral movements or changes in wallet control; the cited tracking alone does not distinguish them. Nor does it establish how much mining revenue, if any, was realised and spent on wages. Reported peak and current balances differ across trackers and dates, so this article gives no September 2026 sovereign balance or realised-profit estimate. The distinction matters: producing bitcoin with public energy, holding it as a corporate asset, selling it for budget revenue and placing it in central-bank reserves are four different policies. [Druk Holding and Investments mining announcement](https://www.dhi.bt/newsroom/news/69455944151590a78f28ad59).
 
## The rest: announcements, tests and regimes
 
**Czech Republic.** Governor Aleš Michl floated up to 5% of reserves in January 2025; the ECB's president said publicly that no central bank in the EU's General Council would hold bitcoin. On 13 November 2025 the CNB bought a $1 million test portfolio — bitcoin, dollar stablecoins and a tokenised deposit — booked as an intangible asset outside official reserves, to be evaluated over two to three years. Its own analysis found a 5% allocation would have raised returns 3.5 points a year and doubled volatility; a 1% allocation would raise return at roughly unchanged risk. It is the only central-bank purchase in history.
 
**Pakistan.** A Crypto Council was formed in early 2025; at a Las Vegas conference on 29 May 2025 its chairman announced a government-led Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and 2,000 megawatts for mining and AI. The IMF objected within days and rejected the subsidised electricity tariff in July 2025. A Virtual Assets Act passed in March 2026 and licensing opened in August 2026; the reserve remains at the planning stage with no disclosed holdings. Pakistan ranks third in the world in grassroots adoption.
 
**Kazakhstan.** A state crypto fund launched in September 2025 (its first reported purchase was Binance's token); central-bank investment and a reserve funded by seized or mined coins have been proposed. A tracker attribution of about 3,500 bitcoin is not a verified, dated public balance or proof that those coins sit in official reserves.
 
**Brazil.** A bill to place up to 5% of reserves (one million coins) in bitcoin was reintroduced in February 2026; the central bank opposes it and it remains in committee. **Ukraine** has a bill permitting the central bank to hold virtual assets, not adopted; the "46,000 coins" attributed to Ukraine in trackers are officials' declared private holdings, not state reserves. **Switzerland**'s constitutional initiative to force the SNB to hold bitcoin lapsed in 2026 with half the required signatures; the SNB's chairman said crypto does not meet the requirements of a reserve asset. **Poland**'s central-bank governor said in February 2025 that it would not consider bitcoin "under any circumstances." **Japan** rejected a reserve in December 2024 but in July 2026 passed a law cutting crypto tax from up to 55% to a flat 20% from 2028, opening the way to domestic ETFs; Metaplanet, a Tokyo-listed company, holds 43,000 coins. **Hong Kong** listed spot ETFs in April 2024. **The UAE**'s sovereign funds hold about $764 million of the BlackRock ETF and the state mines through Citadel Mining.
 
**Sellers and unresolved seizures.** Germany's state of Saxony sold seized bitcoin in 2024; a completed sale is different from either a wallet outflow or an unsold seizure. UK prosecutors report recovery of about 61,000 bitcoin in the Jian Wen money-laundering case and ongoing High Court civil-recovery proceedings. Recovery into official custody is not a final determination that the Treasury owns those coins or holds them as a reserve. [UK Crown Prosecution Service case update](https://www.cps.gov.uk/node/29684).
 
**Users by necessity.** Russia legalised mining and crypto for foreign trade in 2024, and a law signed on 4 August 2026 (effective 1 September) licenses exchanges while keeping the ban on domestic payments; a rouble stablecoin, A7A5, moved $93 billion in under a year, and Duma leaders say crypto "will never become money in Russia" even as exporters use it. Venezuela's state oil company has required dollar stablecoins for spot crude since 2024 after Tether froze its wallets; its own Petro token, launched 2018, was shut in 2024. Iran's central bank on 9 September 2026 allowed importers to settle in stablecoins and bitcoin through domestic exchanges — a de facto endorsement of crypto for sanctions evasion. North Korea "adopts" bitcoin by theft: $2 billion stolen in 2025, three-quarters of it from the Bybit exchange in a single February attack, funding its weapons programmes.

## What the record shows
 
The cases illustrate why legal form, actual payment use, and public-asset holdings need separate evaluation. El Salvador ended mandatory private acceptance without repealing the law's “curso legal” label; the Central African Republic removed its legal-tender provision under a different monetary-union constraint. El Salvador's adoption rates, remittance costs and economic effects need their own source and causal checks; the 2025 amendment alone establishes none of them. Holdings from seizures, mining and purchases also have different fiscal implications. [El Salvador, Decree 199](https://www.jurisprudencia.gob.sv/DocumentosBoveda/R/2/2020-2029/2021/06/1080B6.HTML?embedded=true); [IMF Country Report 25/58, para. 27 n.18](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1slvea2025001-print-pdf.pdf).
 
## Key takeaways
 
El Salvador provides a test of mandatory Bitcoin acceptance alongside an existing dollar unit of account, not of an economy fully denominated in Bitcoin. The 2025 law was **amended, not repealed**: private acceptance became voluntary, tax payment in Bitcoin was no longer authorized, and the domestic text retained “curso legal” wording even as the IMF described the essential features of legal tender as removed. The law was approved in January but took effect 90 days after its 30 January publication. Whether the programme's separate limits on public holdings and wallet operations were fulfilled requires evidence beyond the amended statute.
