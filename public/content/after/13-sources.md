# 13 — Sources and Further Reading

## Gold and reserve-basket currencies (reviewed 13 September 2026)

IMF, *Financial Organization and Operations of the IMF*, sixth ed. (2001), ch. II, “Gold in the Articles of Agreement” — https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/pam/pam45/pdf/chap2.pdf . Distinguishes the 1978 end of the international gold par-value system from continued official gold reserves.

Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe, *2024 Monetary Policy Statement*, “Anchor of the Currency” — https://www.rbz.co.zw/index.php/monetary-policy/monetary-policy-statements/1389-2024-monetary-policy-statement ; IMF, *Zimbabwe: 2025 Article IV Consultation—Informational Annex*, “Exchange Rate Arrangements” — https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1zweea2025001-source-pdf.pdf . ZiG's announced reserve basket includes gold and foreign currency, while its exchange-rate arrangement is classified as de jure floating and de facto other managed; neither source establishes a classical fixed-gold-weight currency.

## Money creation and asset purchases (reviewed 13 September 2026)

Bank of England, *Money creation in the modern economy* (Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1), Figures 1–3 and pp. 16–20, 24–25 — https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/2014/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy.pdf . Distinguishes loan-created deposits, interbank reserves, constraints on lending and the balance-sheet effects of asset purchases from non-bank sellers.

UK Debt Management Office, *Investor Information* — https://www.dmo.gov.uk/investor-information/ . Gilt issuance finances government needs and refinances maturing debt; it is separate from a central-bank purchase of an existing gilt.

Federal Reserve History, *The Great Recession*, “Monetary Policy Response” — https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/great-recession-of-200709 . First U.S. large-scale asset-purchase programme, including approximately $1.75 trillion in longer-term purchases.

[Élysée, Rambouillet declaration of 17 November 1975](https://www.elysee.fr/valery-giscard-d-estaing/1975/11/17/declaration-commune-des-chefs-detat-et-de-gouvernement-de-la-republique-federale-dallemagne-des-etats-unis-damerique-de-france-du-royaume-uni-de-grande-bretagne-et-dirlande-du-nord-d-italie-et-du-japon-reunis-pour-le-premier-sommet-des-pays-ind) locates six-country monetary-reform discussion before the January 1976 Jamaica agreement, not a summit convened to bless that completed accord.

[IMF institutional history, *Collapse of the Par Value System*, chapter 25](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9781451971477/ch025.xml), passage beginning “By August 15”: liabilities to foreign official authorities exceeded US official reserves by almost $30 billion; the following US notification records suspension of free gold purchases and sales for international settlement under the old Articles. This is a named liability/reserve comparison, not a four-to-one dollar-holdings/gold-stock ratio, proof of a $3 billion British request, or a single causal account. [Federal Reserve History, “Nixon Ends Convertibility”](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/gold-convertibility-ends), opening and Camp David decision paragraphs, describes looming gold-run and inflation concerns. [Federal Reserve History, “The Great Inflation”](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/great-inflation), late-1960s passage, identifies Vietnam and Great Society fiscal strain alongside monetary accommodation; it does not isolate Vietnam as the cause of closure.

[Federal Reserve History, “The Smithsonian Agreement”](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/smithsonian-agreement), paragraphs beginning “The Smithsonian Agreement did little...” and “On February 12, 1973,” records interventions, unwanted official dollar accumulations, further devaluation and subsequent major-currency floats; it does not prove a sole failure cause or trader foresight. [IMF, *The Impact of Higher Oil Prices on the Economy*, Appendix](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/oil/2000/index.htm) and [Federal Reserve History, “Oil Shock of 1973–74”](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/oil-shock-of-1973-74) locate oil-related terms-of-trade and inflation pressure after floating began, not the oil shock as the necessary cause of 1971–73 changes.

[IMF Annual Report 1978](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/ar/archive/pdf/ar1978.pdf), “Gold” and “Special Drawing Rights,” states that the Second Amendment removed the IMF official gold price and member gold-payment obligation, allowed members to transact and account for gold subject to domestic law, and changed SDR valuation. [IMF, *Financial Organization and Operations*, chapter II](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9781589060678/ch02.xml), “Gold in the Articles of Agreement,” specifies removal of gold from par values and SDR valuation and continued limited IMF gold transactions. [IMF, “Gold in the IMF”](https://www.imf.org/en/about/factsheets/sheets/2022/gold-in-the-imf), “The role of gold” and “How does the IMF use the gold it has?”, says gold remains an official reserve asset. These sources support “reduced formal IMF role,” not a general ban on reserve gold or the phrase “just a commodity.”

Bank for International Settlements, *History of the Basel Committee* — https://www.bis.org/bcbs/history.htm

## 1973–1982: oil, petrodollars, stagflation

[IMF, *The Impact of Higher Oil Prices on the Economy* (2000), Appendix, Figures A2–A3 and Table A1](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/oil/2000/index.htm) estimates the 1973–74 oil-shock direct terms-of-trade loss at around 2½% of **OECD GDP**, not an annual share of world GDP. [IMF, *The IMF in a Changing World, 1945–85*, “Economic Turbulence of the 1970s”](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9780939934652/ch014.xml) describes the 1974–75 international recession, price pressure and stagnation; it does not establish that all Keynesian models ruled out stagflation. [IMF Annual Report 1976](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/ar/archive/pdf/ar1976.pdf) records full drawing of the first in May 1976. [IMF, *Official Policy to Encourage Requests*, UK arrangement section](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9781451965025/ch02.xml) locates the 15 December 1976 letter of intent and two-year 1977 approval. ## Wars and money

[Costs of War, Neta C. Crawford, *US Budgetary Costs of the Post-9/11 Wars* (September 2021)](https://costsofwar.watson.brown.edu/sites/default/files/papers/Costs-of-War_US-Budgetary-Costs-of-Post-9-11-Wars.pdf), Table 1 pp. 6–7 and financing discussion p. 8: **$5.843tn** estimated war-related spending through FY2022 plus about **$2.2tn** projected future veterans' care through FY2050, for a **$8.043tn composite** including overseas operations, interest, base-budget increases and homeland security.
