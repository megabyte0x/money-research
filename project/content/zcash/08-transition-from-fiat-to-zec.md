# 08 — What Would a Transition from Fiat to ZEC Look Like?

**Status: conditional pathway, not a forecast.** This chapter starts from today's mixed monetary world. The fully supportive political counterfactual is developed separately in [11](11-hypothesis-a-world-ready-for-zec.md) and [12](12-hypothesis-adoption-roadmap-and-worked-example.md).

## First choose what is being adopted

A government or business can adopt Zcash as a payment rail, hold ZEC as an asset, or form obligations in ZEC. These are separate decisions. A useful transition does not require announcing the final outcome before testing the earlier steps.

## Phase A — Make lawful private settlement usable

Build reliable acquisition and cash-out routes, interoperable wallets, transaction records, recovery procedures, and merchant support. Measure fees and spreads end to end. Test the experience on low-cost devices and under poor connectivity. Institutional signing needs separation of duties and tested support for the current shielded pool.

Prices can remain in fiat during this phase. That is meaningful progress on payment privacy, but it should be reported as such.

**Advance only if:** repeat users obtain a measurable benefit and failures, losses, and disclosure risks are acceptable for the specific use case.

## Phase B — Develop retained working balances

Some businesses begin keeping ZEC for upcoming payments rather than converting every receipt immediately. A firm earns and spends ZEC within a network of suppliers and workers. Treasury policy limits how much unhedged exposure it accepts.

Natural matching matters. A business earning ZEC but owing rent, taxes, and all inputs in dollars remains exposed to the exchange rate. A business with most receipts and operating costs in ZEC has less of that mismatch, although its real costs and foreign purchases can still change.

**Advance only if:** balances and repeat payments persist beyond promotional incentives and are not merely speculative accumulation.

## Phase C — Offer genuinely ZEC-denominated contracts

Start with short contracts: service invoices, modest payroll components, subscriptions, or supply agreements. State the unit, payment timing, fee responsibility, dispute process, and treatment of failed or delayed settlement.

If parties want purchasing-power protection, specify the index or conversion clause. A contract continuously indexed to dollars is not pure ZEC denomination. A contract indexed to a goods basket uses a composite measuring system even when it settles in ZEC.

**Advance only if:** participants voluntarily renew native contracts, can plan cash flows, and do not immediately rewrite them into fiat during moderate shocks.

## Phase D — Build accounting and finance around the unit

Develop ZEC income statements, audited custody, receivables finance, insurance, term lending, and liquid short-term instruments. Build transparent reference-rate methodology with multiple sources and fallback rules. Distinguish a ZEC lending rate from a provider's token promotion or an unhedged fiat yield translated into ZEC.

A consumer-price index can show the ZEC cost of a representative basket. Publishing the index measures purchasing power; it does not stabilise it. Households need usable real-income comparisons, not merely a ZEC chart.

**Advance only if:** finance works through a downturn, auditors can verify complete obligations, and contract enforcement is credible.

## Phase E — Public-sector denomination

If society chooses to proceed, assess selected taxes and public procurement in ZEC, pay corresponding expenditures in ZEC, and publish the budget in that unit. This creates recurring demand and helps align the accounting network.

Tax acceptance at a floating conversion rate is an earlier, weaker measure. Real tax denomination means a liability of, for example, 1 ZEC remains 1 ZEC unless the tax formula changes; the state does not continuously recalculate it to preserve a pre-existing fiat amount.

The state must acquire spending balances from taxation, borrowing, assets, or transfers. Adopting a public blockchain does not give a national government ownership of its existing coins or authority to issue more.

**Advance only if:** public reserves, tax collection, social protection, and debt service remain viable under adverse ZEC and external-currency scenarios.

## Phase F — Broader standard, if the evidence supports it

Only after the earlier functions are durable would a society consider making ZEC its predominant unit. This may never be desirable. A successful privacy payment system can remain successful without reaching this phase.

The eventual monetary arrangement could be:

- Native ZEC with floating external exchange rates.
- National denominations defined as fixed quantities of ZEC.
- Fully reserved ZEC claims used for convenience.
- A hybrid that retains another accounting unit for some contracts.

These alternatives have different legal and balance-sheet effects. A fiat currency merely holding ZEC in its reserves is not a ZEC standard.

## The difficult part: legacy balance sheets

Changing software is easier than changing obligations. Existing mortgages, pensions, bank deposits, sovereign bonds, employment contracts, and foreign invoices must be treated explicitly.

| Obligation | Plausible treatment | Cost or limit |
|---|---|---|
| New short contracts | Voluntary denomination in ZEC | Participants need informed consent and liquidity |
| Existing domestic contracts | Grandfather them, or negotiate conversion under clear law | Conversion creates valuation and distribution disputes |
| Foreign-law debt | Honour original terms unless creditors agree to restructure | Domestic legislation cannot simply erase external currency risk |
| Bank deposits | Transfer only with matching assets, capital, and redemption arrangements | A renamed deposit does not create base ZEC |
| Pensions and insurance | Match liabilities to assets; disclose any indexation or benefit change | Long-duration purchasing-power risk remains |
| Taxes due from earlier periods | Publish transition valuation and settlement rules | Avoid arbitrary retroactive conversion burdens |

## What not to do with exchange rates

“One ZEC equals a fixed number of dollars forever” is a peg, not merely a unit choice. A central authority must be ready to buy and sell at that rate and absorb reserve losses. Holding volatile ZEC against a fixed dollar redemption promise creates a mismatch.

By contrast, defining a new domestic unit as a fixed fraction of ZEC is a denomination choice. Its dollar value floats with ZEC. That does not protect importers or foreign-currency borrowers.

## Distribution and timing

An announced state purchase can move the market before the state accumulates reserves. Market capitalisation is not a pool of cash available for conversion, and a displayed price is not the price at which an entire economy can acquire ZEC.

A fair transition would require an acquisition policy, transparent costs, protection against insider advantage, access for low-income households, and voluntary treatment of existing contracts where feasible. These are real design choices even in a politically supportive world.

## Key takeaways

A credible transition moves from useful settlement to matched economic activity, then to contracts and fiscal denomination if evidence warrants it. The binding work is aligning obligations, assets, and emergency arrangements—not replacing currency labels on a screen.
